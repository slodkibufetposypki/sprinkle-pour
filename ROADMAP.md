# Zen Grains — what is next

## Open
- **Zen mode, the next pass** (from the analysis of endless games: Mini Metro, Tetris Effect, Dorfromantik, Fruit Ninja, Block Blast, Geometry Dash, Subway Surfers, Balatro, Peggle; and the owner's ideas). Built in phases:
  1. **The run itself (done 26 Oct):** pressure that never stops rising (the jar's gap halves about every four minutes); stages every two minutes; the beans change as you go and get grander, each kind worth more per grain (lentils and rice; mung and stones; chickpeas and sesame; moonseeds and kidney beans; star beans and ember beans; nebula beans; then the absurd ones: neon, crystal, a tiny galaxy inside), never more than three to sort at once, a kind leaving has its bowl packed and the new one takes its corner; **the golden bean**, now and then, hopping about and leaping off after a few seconds, always gatherable whatever the sticks are after, a large bonus; the multiplier slowly cools without full rows; bowls of 30 grains. Aim: about 3–5 minutes for a new player, 10–15 for a good one, 25+ for a master.
  1b. **Zen, second pass (done 26 Oct, after the owner played it: too slow, too easy, things happening without saying why):** scoops are mixed (two kinds after 30 s, three after three minutes) and grow from a row's worth to two; the jar pours 1.6 grains a second, a quarter more every minute; stages last 90 s. Only a full board costs a life; a broken row costs points, the multiplier and a quarter of the Zen Zone. **The Zen Zone:** a meter under the multiplier, charged by full rows (14 or so); when full, for 8 s the jar pours faster, wrong grains sleep and break nothing, every row pays double. Stones sink into the sand after 22 s. The capybara's five bowls are shown as five pips under the meter; a rules card on the first zen run (and in the pause menu); one-line tips the first time a bowl fills, the zone charges, a stone sinks. A kind now hands its corner on only once none of it is left in the jar either (before, a scoop still in the jar could land with no bowl to go to). Measured with `tools/sim/zenstats.mjs`: the board is a third full on average (it was 5%), the bot lasts 9–10 minutes.
  1c. **The night's rhythm (done 26 Oct; the owner: "it pours, I gather, it pours, I gather"):** between stretches of steady pouring the jar does something, every 20–30 s and closer together later, never the same twice running: a **wave** (2 s of the jar shaking, then 12 s of hard pouring with every kind mixed, then a 9 s **calm** to tidy up), a **spill** (from the second stage: the jar tips over and a mix lands across the whole board) or an **order** (one bowl asks for so many grains of its kind in 22 s; a ring at the bowl shows what is in and the time left; filled, it pays and charges a third of the Zen Zone; missed, it takes some back). Measured: runs of 4–10 minutes for the bot, orders filled about 70% of the time.
  2. **Zen Capybara** (the owner's idea, in place of the cat here): a very relaxed, meditating capybara. Every few bowls it appears, bows, thanks you for the bowls and looks on you kindly: a life back (or, with all three, a blessing in points). At game over it can give one more life to carry on (bought later; costs 1, then 2, then 4 capybaras in one run, at most three; runs with a continue are marked on score boards; no countdown on the offer).
  3. **What you see grow:** zen keeps its raked sand (the owner, 26 Oct), and as the stages go by its furrows begin to glow, faintly at first and a little more at every stage, with a band of light running up through them when a stage begins (done 26 Oct); the chopsticks' tips glow and spark, then whole sticks are covered in runes of our own that kindle and burn like an old enchanted ring; at ×10, rose petals and a moment of slow motion; the score glows; a quiet line for your best and "New best!" mid-run, and a stage banner (both done 26 Oct).
  4. **The end as a tower of bowls:** every bowl of the run is stacked on a stone on the moss of a forest floor; the tower grows and the view climbs with it, through flowers and moss, bushes, young trees, tall trees, birds in flight, the dark, fog, sunrise, above the treetops and into the clouds and beyond, so the height of the tower shows how good the run was. Then the score.
  5. Later: the Zen Zone (a meter filled by full rows: the jar stops, wrong grains freeze, every row counts full), zen ranks, a lifetime count, daily goals, unlockable looks, a jar of the week (one shared seed), a share card and a 30-second replay for streamers.
- **A mode for children (the owner's idea, to decide):** what zen was at first, gathering at an easy pace, but with a point to it. Proposed: no lives and no clock, a slow jar, one kind at a time, bigger reach; every full bowl feeds an animal of a small garden round the board (a capybara family, a cat, a hamster), which then appears and stays; a garden full of animals opens the next garden. Nothing to lose, something to make.
- **The workshop (the owner's idea, for later):** at some point in the game a workshop opens, where for points you build a new pair of chopsticks by joining two pairs you already own. The new pair carries both bonuses (e.g. cherry and ebony: the flash when the kind changes and slower wrong grains), and has a look of its own made from the two. To decide when it is built: what the points are (spent from score, sorted grains, or a currency of its own), when the workshop opens, whether the two pairs used are kept, and whether a joined pair can be joined again.
- **Mini-games now and then, to break the routine** (the owner's sister): stacking stones into a cairn with real physics (shapes, friction, balance); a pull-the-pin puzzle (beans held behind latches, released in the right order into the right bowls); a matching puzzle; copying a bean pattern in the zen sand. One at a time, the cairn first.
- **The result layout:** a card at the top is the default; `?done=card` and `?done=sheet` show the other two. Pick one after playing.
- **Hard levels:** mark every fourth or fifth level "hard" (a tighter clock), tuned from real players' times rather than guessed.
- **The room and its upgrades:** restoring and decorating the hut with stars (asset production, later).
- **Room by time of day:** morning, day and evening versions of the room. Each needs a re-lit image and a video from Higgsfield; the room's loop is made with `tools/media/room_loop.py`.
- **Daily board:** waiting on a choice: a share button (score sent by message), or a shared score board (needs an online service).
- **Music:** waiting on the owner.
- **A rule of Paper's own** (optional), e.g. grains slowly sliding into the fold.
- **Original fantasy content** (in the spirit of Ghibli and Zelda, never their names, characters or designs):
  - **The magic scroll (a board):** an unrolled scroll on wooden rollers, an ink landscape on it that glows faintly. On the scroll every grain floats: they all drift slowly on their own and glide far when pushed, so a gathered heap never stays put.
  - **Moonseeds (a grain):** light and pale, they come in with the scroll and then appear on other boards too. Off the scroll only the moonseeds float; everything else lies still as usual.
  - **Ember beans:** they glow warm and cool down as the round goes on, and are worth more while warm. Now and then one bursts on its own: it glows brighter and trembles for a moment first (time to pick it up), then pops with sparks, scattering the grains around it, and lies there as a cooled, ordinary bean.
  - Also proposed: a rune stone board (glowing lines that carry grains along them), spirit moss (soft, grains hardly roll), night glass (slippery, pushed grains slide far), acorns (roll and spin when pushed).
  - Where they go: as boards after Paper and before the endless one. That moves the endless board's levels, which saves keep by number, so the save needs a mapping when they are added.
- **Porcelain with gold seams (a board, the owner's idea, design shown 26 Oct):** a white porcelain tray with a gold rim, mended kintsugi-style with fine gold seams, and a hand-painted grey-green ink scene at one edge with a single animal in full colour (concepts: a meditating capybara with a yuzu in a stream; koi under lotus leaves). Proposed rule: the glaze is smooth, so pushed grains slide far, and the raised gold seams stop them. Proposed: every level of the board a different plate of the same service, each with its own animal. Needs: an ink-and-gold HUD on light boards (chalk white vanishes on white), a fine outline on rice and other pale grains, the painting kept to the edges and printed a little paler than the concept.
- **Carrying rows yourself, if testers find it tedious** (the owner's worry): keep it to the parchment board only; a larger drop area; a flick toward the pile instead of carrying it there; or two rows carried at once for a bonus.

## For the App Store launch
- **Model:** first three boards free; one purchase (about 14.99 PLN) opens the rest, every board added later, and "open this board now" for a stuck player. No ads; at most optional rewarded ones, never between levels.
- **No stars for sale:** boards open at 70% of the stars before them, and selling stars would read as raising the bar to sell the way over it; consumable currency sold to children is also what stores and consumer authorities watch most.
- **Chopsticks with abilities (in the game now, earned by playing):** cherry lacquer flashes the kind to gather for a moment when it changes, mother-of-pearl (120 stars) keeps it lit all the time, ebony slows wrong grains by 20%, jade reaches 10% further, steel keeps stones back, gold leaf eases the pace line. Later: bought as a shortcut, off on the daily board and wherever scores are compared.
- **Helpers (in the game now, earned by playing):** the Zen Cat turns the clock back once a round, and is the first offer when the time is up; the Zen Hamster carries the stones off once a round. Three cats to start, a cat for a level's first five stars, two cats and two hamsters when a board opens. Later: sold in small packs, never offered to a child without the parent check.
- **Gentle setting, free:** slower wrong grains, weaker stones, a softer clock, for children and anyone stuck; levels played with it are marked.
- **Also for sale:** material packs (looks only), a supporter pack / tip jar.
- **Children:** a parent check before any price, no countdowns or "only today".
- **What the launch needs:** an Apple developer account (and Google Play's), the game wrapped as a store app (it already installs and works offline), purchases through StoreKit / Play Billing (15% for small developers), privacy labels and an age rating. A wrapper that is only a website can be refused; offline play, purchases and haptics make it an app.
