# Zen Grains — what is next

## The plan (27 Oct, after the design review)
**Done 28 Oct (the owner's calls):**
- **A missed order** costs only the capybara's blessing (the bowls counted toward it start again) and points: 15 for each grain it asked for, times what the kind is worth. No life, and the Zen Zone keeps its charge.
- **The player fires the Zen Zone.** Full, the tube holds its charge: a broken row and an idle spell no longer take from it. It glows gold and a gold finger bobs above it; a tap on the tube starts the ten seconds. The first time, one tip says so.
- **Names:** the levels mode is **Droga** ("The Way"); Pełnia is the endless mode with lives; Zen is kept for the calm mode.
- **Type:** text in Kaisei Decol, titles in Yuji Syuku (variant A). Numerals: the score in a thick brush (Potta One), everything else that counts (multiplier, points, totals) in Yuji Syuku; the time and the small counts (the Zone countdown, the dial, the order, the jar, the helpers) in Yuji Syuku made bolder, so the thin strokes hold at 16–18 px.
- **Pełnia's multiplier** is earned over minutes: a full row adds a quarter of what it does in a level and less the higher it stands (about ×5 after a minute of clean quick rows, ×9 after three). Scoops are mixed after the first two and scattered, not laid out as ready rows.
- **No farming the edge:** grains pressed into a wall or a bowl are squeezed out and leap across the board; picking a line of grains off an edge, or sweeping the grains into a long thin line anywhere on the board, brings the Zen Cat's paw, which stirs the line across the board and wags a claw (no, no, no). Only grains the sticks moved there count, so a level's own shapes never call her; once every 20 s at most.
- **Droga:** a carried row can be thrown: let go mid-swing toward a bowl.
- **A full board shows itself:** the next ensō reddens and trembles as the board fills, a ring round it runs out the three seconds once it is full, the board's edge glows red, and one line says it.
- **The Pełnia prototype is built:** a lunar month (a minute a phase, the first full moon at four minutes), the light from a dark new moon to a silver full one, a drifting pool of moonlight that pays up to ×2 for rows closed in it, the phase as a small moon by the capybara, and the blood moon every third full moon (×3 on rows and orders, twice the stones, the jar a quarter faster, no capybara; getting through it gives a life back). Next: the owner plays it.
- **Heat on an iPhone (Ania, 17 Pro Max, Safari):** the game logic was never the cost (about 5% of a frame); painting was. The board is now drawn at two device pixels per CSS pixel instead of three, at most 60 frames a second in play and 30 in the menus while the camera holds still (the room had copied every video frame to the canvas at the screen's full rate), without blurred canvas shadows (the sticks and the points), and with the moonlight in one pass instead of two. Measured with a CPU profile at a Pro Max size: a frame of Pełnia costs about a third of what it did.
- **Higgsfield anchors made** (17.5 credits): the capybara's turnaround sheet F01 (in `art/`), the six medallions A03 (in the game), lacquer and washi A25 (the washi is good, the lacquer muddy; neither used yet). The capybara's idle sprite through AutoSprite failed on the service's side twice, unbilled.

**Names (the owner, 27 Oct):** the endless mode with lives becomes **Pełnia** (Full Moon). **Zen** is kept for the calm mode without lives or a clock (below). The levels mode still needs a name: proposed **Droga** ("the Way", as in the way of tea), or Ścieżka, or plain Plansze.

**Pełnia: a run is a lunar month** (the owner's idea, 27 Oct; the shape proposed, nothing built yet):
- Each phase is one turn of the jar's rhythm, about a minute. New moon: lanterns only, a calm start, one stone. Crescent: the first silver light, a pool of moonlight appears. First quarter: half the board silver, high tide, grains drift slowly one way. Gibbous: more stones, the moonseeds come. **Full moon:** the whole board silver; the peak, with the biggest wave, the capybara's moon-viewing, and everything paying more. Waning: the light fades, fog and the ebb, stones surface from the sand. Then a second new moon, darker and faster.
- The first full moon comes at about 4–5 minutes: a new player reaches the first quarter, a good one sees the full moon and the second month, a master meets the blood moon. The best run reads as how far the moon got, not only as points.
- **Blood moon,** every third full moon: red light, twice the stones, the capybara hides, everything pays ×3. Clearly harder than the rest, so that getting through it satisfies (the owner, 27 Oct); it gives something rare.
- Mechanics to try: a pool of moonlight drifting across the sand, rows gathered inside it pay more (the first real choice in the mode: gather where it is easy or where it shines); tides through the tilt the lacquer board already has; fog in the waning phases that never hides a grain's outline; moonseeds brighter and worth more as the moon grows, floating at the full moon; stones rising with the tide, so the one-two-three of stones follows the moon. A choice of one of three blessings at each new moon.
- Folklore to draw on (public, nobody's property): tsukimi, moon-viewing with rice dumplings (dango) stacked as an offering, and the rabbit in the moon pounding rice into mochi. The end of a run could be the run's bowls stacked like dango as an offering, in place of or beside the tower of bowls.
- Later: the real moon's phase (computed on the device), with a special jar on real full-moon nights, the same for everyone.
- Its own light: the levels stay candlelit; Pełnia is a garden at night, a silver moon from the upper right, warm lanterns as an accent, gold only for rewards. The Zen Zone stays the reward for quick rows, its tube filled with silver sand; the small moon in the top band shows only the phase.
- First step: a prototype on the current mode with no new rules (the phases change the light, the pool of moonlight pays more, a small moon at the top), checked with the bot, then played by the owner.

**The design review (27 Oct):** three reviewers, each with a different design skill (frontend-design, dataviz, canvas-design), went through every screen of both modes. Their findings, a table of 36 assets with ready Higgsfield prompts, and the style guide are on the page "Pracownia Zen Grains" (https://claude.ai/artifact/Ru9WhKoWKU47USHNyKHhpc). The game reads as four games glued together (a painted hut, a flat procedural board, dialog cards from a dark web app, CGI medallions); the one direction to join them is a candlelit still life: painted objects on one table, information in ink, only gold and flame glowing. Measured: in Pełnia 17 things crowd the top 200 px; on a level with a clock a grain lay under the HUD in 47 of 80 samples (41 under the cat), because the board starts at 119 px and the left column reaches 204 px.

**The order of work:**
1. **Polish without new art** (the review's fixes 1–16; done 28 Oct in five passes, except the hamster's and the cat's muted state until they are useful, the gold-leaf inlay on the gold and lacquer sticks, and the ember's light on the board beside the sticks): the Zen Zone bar flashing red all the time while you wait (a bug: the idle drain fires the loss flash every 0.3 s); one band at the top (back and again only in the pause menu, the multiplier shown from ×1.1, the best only when near it, touches passing through to the board); grains pushed out from under the HUD, the jar and the bar (keep-out rectangles in the sim, as the bowls already have); one message system in three tiers (tip, event, big moment written into the sand), never cut off; the cards as a black lacquer tray with the start screen's plaque as the main button; the Zen Zone as a bamboo tube of sand that pours out like an hourglass while it lasts; the clock as a small dish with the cat on it; one gold, red only for a loss, × only for points; the script carved into the sticks with the ember in the groove; lives as ensō with the capybara's eight bowls as a ring; two motion curves instead of the overshoot; two typefaces; the start menu; shorter copy; the zen sand's furrows and the sticks' shadow.
2. **Three style anchors from Higgsfield** (the capybara's turnaround sheet F01, the medallions A03, lacquer and washi A25), checked in the game before anything else is generated; then the second wave (the tube, the dish, ensō, bowls in four glazes, the jar, tags, plaques).
3. **The Pełnia prototype** (above), then the lunar month as the run's chapters, the blood moon and the blessings.
4. **The end of a run:** the capybara's scene, the offer to carry on, the offering of bowls.
5. **Between runs:** full bowls as sacks of grain (the currency; points stay the measure of skill), stars restoring the hut (as in Gardenscapes), three missions at a time and a rank, a lifetime count of grains, the capybara's guide behind a "?" (an ink animation before a round with something new instead of a text card, the chopsticks' goals as a picture and a counter, tips from your own last round, three cards instead of the 121-word rules).
6. **The world:** the hut as the menu (modes, workshop, upgrades), its upgrades as separate layers of one painting, the farm on the hill (rice and bean fields, harvests, sacks to sell, helpers to hire), the workshop joining three pairs of chopsticks into one with three bonuses.
7. **Together:** the full-moon jar for everyone, a share card, a replay. In parallel: recording testers' sessions, and music that follows the rhythm.

**Still to decide:** the currency (proposed: sacks); continues (proposed: once free from the capybara, then medallions at 1, 2, 4); the spill's visibly tipping jar; the domain; the result layout; the rest under "Ideas to decide" below.

## Open
- **Zen mode (now Pełnia), the next pass** (from the analysis of endless games: Mini Metro, Tetris Effect, Dorfromantik, Fruit Ninja, Block Blast, Geometry Dash, Subway Surfers, Balatro, Peggle; and the owner's ideas). Built in phases:
  1. **The run itself (done 26 Oct):** pressure that never stops rising (the jar's gap halves about every four minutes); stages every two minutes; the beans change as you go and get grander, each kind worth more per grain (lentils and rice; mung and stones; chickpeas and sesame; moonseeds and kidney beans; star beans and ember beans; nebula beans; then the absurd ones: neon, crystal, a tiny galaxy inside), never more than three to sort at once, a kind leaving has its bowl packed and the new one takes its corner; **the golden bean**, now and then, hopping about and leaping off after a few seconds, always gatherable whatever the sticks are after, a large bonus; the multiplier slowly cools without full rows; bowls of 30 grains. Aim: about 3–5 minutes for a new player, 10–15 for a good one, 25+ for a master.
  1b. **Zen, second pass (done 26 Oct, after the owner played it: too slow, too easy, things happening without saying why):** scoops are mixed (two kinds after 30 s, three after three minutes) and grow from a row's worth to two; the jar pours 1.6 grains a second, a quarter more every minute; stages last 90 s. Only a full board costs a life; a broken row costs points, the multiplier and a quarter of the Zen Zone. **The Zen Zone:** a meter under the multiplier, charged by full rows (14 or so); when full, for 8 s (10 s since 27 Oct, with rows flying to their bowls by themselves) the jar pours faster, wrong grains sleep and break nothing, every row pays double. Stones sink into the sand after 22 s. The capybara's five bowls are shown as five pips under the meter; a rules card on the first zen run (and in the pause menu); one-line tips the first time a bowl fills, the zone charges, a stone sinks. The meter shows why it fills: each full row (a short row: a smaller one) and each filled order sends a gold mote up into it, and the bar fills as the mote lands; what a broken row or a missed order takes away flashes red (the owner asked how the zone charges, 26 Oct). A full bowl rushes off the board with its beans (a beat back, then away with streaks of air and a whoosh: low and full, its energy around 700 Hz where the chopsticks' swish sits near 2.8 kHz, as loud as a full row's chime) and an empty one slides into the corner and is set down with a small knock; a mote from it fills one of five little bowls beside the capybara's medallion, which took the place of a drawn face that read as a hamster (the owner, 26 Oct). A kind now hands its corner on only once none of it is left in the jar either (before, a scoop still in the jar could land with no bowl to go to). Measured with `tools/sim/zenstats.mjs`: the board is a third full on average (it was 5%), the bot lasts 9–10 minutes.
  1c. **The night's rhythm (done 26 Oct; the owner: "it pours, I gather, it pours, I gather"):** between stretches of steady pouring the jar does something, every 20–30 s and closer together later, never the same twice running: a **wave** (2 s of the jar shaking, then 12 s of hard pouring with every kind mixed, then a 9 s **calm** to tidy up), a **spill** (from the second stage: the jar tips over and a mix lands across the whole board) or an **order** (one bowl asks for so many grains of its kind in 22 s; a ring at the bowl shows what is in and the time left; filled, it pays and charges a third of the Zen Zone; missed, it takes some back). Measured: runs of 4–10 minutes for the bot, orders filled about 70% of the time.
  2. **Zen Capybara** (the owner's idea, in place of the cat here): a very relaxed, meditating capybara. Every few bowls it appears, bows, thanks you for the bowls and looks on you kindly: a life back (or, with all three, a blessing in points). At game over it can give one more life to carry on (bought later; costs 1, then 2, then 4 capybaras in one run, at most three; runs with a continue are marked on score boards; no countdown on the offer).
  3. **What you see grow:** zen keeps its raked sand (the owner, 26 Oct), and as the stages go by its furrows begin to glow, faintly at first and a little more at every stage (done 26 Oct). A new stage is not announced: no name, no list of kinds, no gong; the sand only settles a shade brighter and each new kind says itself when its bowl comes. The gong stays for the end of a round, the board lighting up for the Zen Zone (the owner, 26 Oct); ASMR mode's reward (capybara, golden bean, a run of full rows, an order filled, a new best, a board opening) was a struck bowl that rang like a smaller gong about every 20 s, and is now three soft kalimba notes (the owner, 27 Oct); **the Zen Zone as a build-up and a frenzy (27 Oct; the owner: the runes and the petals at ×10 looked weak and came all at once, and the zone has to really give something, like the frenzy in Fruit Ninja):** full rows fill it in four steps and a break takes it a step back. Each step helps and shows: from the first, the multiplier grows a quarter faster, the chopsticks' tips catch the light and the furrows of the sand breathe; from the second, the multiplier holds 8 s before it cools (5 before) and a script of our own carved down the sticks (bars, slanting bars, a stem with branches, dots, a cross) lights from the tips and burns further toward the hand as the zone fills; from the third, the multiplier grows half again faster and the tips spark like a welder's rod; full, for 10 s, every closed row flies to its bowl by itself while the sticks open again where they are (no trip to the bowl and back, so rows come about 40% faster), rows pay double, the jar pours about twice what can be gathered (4× as fast, scoops 2.2× as big), all the script burns, sparks shower and rose petals fall the whole time; then it fades out over a second or two. **Since the owner's next pass (27 Oct):** wrong grains in the zone no longer sleep but lie still: they do not creep in, yet driving the tips onto one (or a stone) breaks the row and ends the zone at once, so the frenzy is gathering fast while steering round them; what the zone poured and was not gathered sinks into the sand when it ends (a ripple each) and never counts toward a full board; every row in zen sends a ring of light out from its bowl through the furrows of the sand, much quicker in the zone; the bowl's whoosh got about 6 dB louder, then (the owner: too loud) about 5 dB softer again, swelling instead of striking, a little above a full row's chime; the rings of light were slowed to a calm wide wave, about 3.4 s across the board (2 s in the zone), easing off as they spread. Measured with the bot after it: about 4 zones a run, half of them broken, some 30 grains sunk at the end of each. **Then (27 Oct; the owner: it filled after a few cups, the effects growing step by step said nothing, the petals hid the beans):** no petals and no steps. The Zen Zone has an upright meter at the right side of the board, dim with a countdown until it opens at 1:30; quick full rows fill it (a Lightning row 8%, Quick 7%, Steady 4%, Slow 1.5%, a short one nothing), a broken row takes a fifth and four seconds without a row drain it; it glows when nearly full. Full, its name comes up large for a moment with what it gives ("rows fly to their bowls · ×2"), and only then does anything change on the board: the script burns down the sticks from the tips, the tips glow and spark, the sand pulses and the bowls send rings of light through it in turn; it all goes out when the zone ends ("The Zen Zone is over" or "broke"). Measured with the bot: the first zone at about 2:00–2:50 (once 6:17), 1–3 a run. **A stone in a row costs a life (27 Oct; the owner, after his sister's first run of 400,000 with no sense of where it was going):** until then only a full board did, and the bot lost all its lives in the last 20–60 s of a 7–11 minute run, so lives meant nothing for most of it. With stones costing one, the first life goes at 2–5 minutes, runs last about as long, and the capybara gives lives back about three times as often. A wrong grain still only breaks the row: costing a life for it ended the bot's runs within 1–2 minutes. A wrong grain only breaks the row (the owner, 27 Oct; cracks put aside for now). **The rhythm (27 Oct; the owner: stones should come and go, one, two, three, two, and the whole game needs a rhythm):** the jar's events no longer come at random but round and round, about a minute a turn: steady pouring (16 s, shorter each stage), an order, a 5 s breath, the peak (a wave, and from the second stage a spill every other time), a calm, and again. Stones follow it from the second stage: one on the board while it is steady, two with the order and in the calm, three at the peak, brought in at once and sinking after 18 s (22 before). Measured with the bot: runs of 2.5–10 minutes, the first life lost at 1:40–6:10, nearly always to a stone. The bar shows the steps as notches, and two soft plucks mark each step up. Measured with the bot: 5–6 zones in a run, points per second in the zone about 2.7× what they were, runs as long as before with scores 30–50% higher. (The first try, runes lit by the multiplier and petals with slow motion at ×10, is gone.) The score glows warmer as the zone fills; a quiet line for your best and "New best!" mid-run (done 26 Oct).
  4. **The end as a tower of bowls:** every bowl of the run is stacked on a stone on the moss of a forest floor; the tower grows and the view climbs with it, through flowers and moss, bushes, young trees, tall trees, birds in flight, the dark, fog, sunrise, above the treetops and into the clouds and beyond, so the height of the tower shows how good the run was. Then the score.

- **A calm mode, for children and for anyone who just wants to sort, to be called Zen (the owner's idea; the name decided 27 Oct; 26 Oct: "chillowy, w nieskończoność, satysfakcjonujący, ASMR: zbieramy, segregujemy, układamy miseczki"):** what zen was at first, gathering at an easy pace, but with a point to it. Proposed: no lives and no clock, a slow jar, one kind at a time, bigger reach; every full bowl feeds an animal of a small garden round the board (a capybara family, a cat, a hamster), which then appears and stays; a garden full of animals opens the next garden. Nothing to lose, something to make.
- **The workshop (the owner's idea, for later):** at some point in the game a workshop opens, where for points you build a new pair of chopsticks by joining two pairs you already own. - **Mini-games now and then, to break the routine** (the owner's sister): stacking stones into a cairn with real physics (shapes, friction, balance); a pull-the-pin puzzle (beans held behind latches, released in the right order into the right bowls); a matching puzzle; copying a bean pattern in the zen sand. One at a time, the cairn first. **Two more from the owner (27 Oct), both themed and both calm:**
  - **The capybaras' hot spring** (from the real baths at Japanese zoos, which began at Izu Shaboten Zoo in 1982 when a keeper saw the capybaras gather in a puddle of warm cleaning water): a logic puzzle on a rocky slope. Route the hot spring's water through bamboo pipes (kakehi) so every capybara waiting below gets its stream: tap to turn a piece (straight, bend, splitter); a split halves the flow; hot and cold streams mix to "just right", shown by the steam; a shishi-odoshi fills, tips with its clack and pours on, as a relay or a timer; a yuzu floats down the pipes and should land on a capybara's head (the third star). No clock; a level is 30–90 s; solved, every capybara shuts its eyes in the steam. Pipes are a small graph, so it is deterministic and a solver can check every level. It takes the place of the pull-the-pin puzzle. Could live by the hut as an onsen to build, and come back as a winter-solstice yuzu bath (tōji).
  - **Otsukimi dango, a game of its own** (the owner, 27 Oct: not in place of the cairn): first cut a log of dough into equal pieces (the hard part: every piece shows its weight, uneven ones make uneven balls); then roll each with a circling thumb (even circles make it round, rushing cracks it); then build the offering for the fifteenth night with the chopsticks, the game's own skill: nine in a 3×3 square, four in a 2×2 square, two on top, the top ones yellow for the moon. The balls are soft and a little slippery, they roll and squash, so uneven ones and a clumsy grip bring the pyramid down. Needs side-on circle physics of our own (deterministic, like the grains). Where: the interlude at a full moon in Pełnia (20–30 s; how well the pyramid stands sets the blessing), the end of a run as an offering, and a yearly moon-viewing night. Optional: pounding mochi with the moon rabbit, in time (strike when it has turned the dough).
  - **The cairn stays a game of its own** (the owner, 27 Oct): stones of different shapes, with hollows and knobs, set so that they lock into one another; you feel each stone's centre of gravity before you let go. Needs real polygon physics (our own or a small vendored engine run at a fixed step, so it stays deterministic); drag to move, twist to turn, the clack of stone on stone. A natural fit for the calm Zen mode.
  - **Threads, a game against the capybara** (the owner's memory of a board game, 27 Oct: it is Triggle, PlayMonster, 2023; the same idea is also sold as triangle chess or triangle takeover): a wooden board of pegs; in turn each player stretches a band over four pegs in a straight line; whoever closes a triangle with the bands already there puts a piece of their colour inside it; most pieces wins. It is dots-and-boxes on a triangular grid, so it has real depth (giving the opponent a triangle, chains and sacrifices). Ours: silk cords on bamboo pegs, beans as the pieces (yours lentils, the capybara's mung beans), drag from a peg along one of the three directions and it snaps to four pegs. The capybara as the opponent at three strengths: easy makes safe moves and the odd slip, normal never hands over a triangle, master searches the endgame. Also two players on one phone. No physics, only rules, so it is cheap, deterministic and testable, with little art (board, pegs, cords, beans, the capybara thinking and pleased). Rules of a game can be taken; its name and look cannot, so the name and art are ours. What is known of Triggle's rules (from shop listings and fan rules, not its rulebook): a band goes straight over four pegs and must touch a band already on the board, so play grows out from the middle; a closed triangle is marked with your peg; the game ends when no triangle is left to close or someone runs out of pieces; with two players about 10 bands each. To settle by playing: the board's size (a hexagon of pegs, as in Triggle), whether closing a triangle gives another move (in dots-and-boxes it does; unknown for Triggle), how cords cross. Before a release, check that the board or the game is not under a patent. Where: at the hut's table in the evening, for sacks or medallions; a match a day.

## For the App Store launch
- **Model:** first three boards free; one purchase (about 14.99 PLN) opens the rest, every board added later, and "open this board now" for a stuck player. No ads; at most optional rewarded ones, never between levels.
- **No stars for sale:** boards open at 70% of the stars before them, and selling stars would read as raising the bar to sell the way over it; consumable currency sold to children is also what stores and consumer authorities watch most.
- **Chopsticks with abilities (in the game now, earned by playing):** cherry lacquer flashes the kind to gather for a moment when it changes, mother-of-pearl (120 stars) keeps it lit all the time, ebony slows wrong grains by 20%, jade reaches 10% further, steel keeps stones back, gold leaf eases the pace line. Later: bought as a shortcut, off on the daily board and wherever scores are compared.
- **Helpers (in the game now, earned by playing):** the Zen Cat turns the clock back once a round, and is the first offer when the time is up; the Zen Hamster carries the stones off once a round. Three cats to start, a cat for a level's first five stars, two cats and two hamsters when a board opens. Later: sold in small packs, never offered to a child without the parent check.
- **Gentle setting, free:** slower wrong grains, weaker stones, a softer clock, for children and anyone stuck; levels played with it are marked.
- **Also for sale:** material packs (looks only), a supporter pack / tip jar.
- **Children:** a parent check before any price, no countdowns or "only today".
- **What the launch needs:** an Apple developer account (and Google Play's), the game wrapped as a store app (it already installs and works offline), purchases through StoreKit / Play Billing (15% for small developers), privacy labels and an age rating. A wrapper that is only a website can be refused; offline play, purchases and haptics make it an app.
